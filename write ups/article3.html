In Memory of My Past

C.J Tongco

There are moments in life when I look back and barely recognize the person I used to be. My past is filled with mistakes, regrets, and choices I wish I could undo. I was not always patient, not always kind, and not always wise. But instead of running from those memories, I choose to honor them. “In Memory of My Past” is not about shame—it is about growth. It is about acknowledging who I was so I can fully appreciate who I am becoming. There was a time when I allowed anger and pride to guide my actions. I hurt people with careless words and pushed away those who only wanted the best for me. I made decisions based on impulse rather than understanding. Looking back, I see how much I had to learn about responsibility and empathy. My past self-did not always know better, but that does not excuse the harm caused. It simply explains the journey I had yet to take. Change did not happen overnight. It came through difficult conversations, quiet reflections, and moments when I had to face uncomfortable truths. I realized that becoming a better person required humility. I had to admit my wrongs, apologize sincerely, and forgive myself as well. Growth meant choosing patience over anger, listening instead of reacting, and learning from every mistake instead of repeating it. As I changed, I began to see the power of small, consistent actions. Being kinder, more understanding, and more mindful reshaped my relationships and my outlook on life. I learned that strength is not about being right all the time but about being willing to improve. The past no longer controls me; instead, it guides me. It reminds me of how far I have come and how much more I can still grow. Today, I carry my past not as a burden but as a lesson. In memory of who I once was, I strive to be better each day. I choose growth over guilt, compassion over pride, and responsibility over excuses. My past shaped me, but it does not define me. It is simply the foundation upon which I continue to build a better version of myself.
