The Devil that Destroys Me

C.J Tongco

When I say “the devil,” I am not talking about a creature in the dark. I am talking about the person I used to be. The immature, selfish version of myself who thought being right was more important than being kind. The one who reacted without thinking and spoke without considering how deeply words can wound. That version of me felt justified in everything. But looking back now, I see how destructive I truly was. I hurt the people who loved me most. I ignored their feelings when they tried to explain how I made them feel. I chose my pride over their peace. Sometimes it was through harsh words. Sometimes it was through silence. Sometimes it was simply not showing up when I should have. I didn’t realize at the time that selfishness isn’t loud and dramatic—it can be quiet and consistent, slowly breaking trust little by little. What hurts the most is knowing that I cannot undo those moments. I can’t take back the tears I caused or the disappointment I placed in their hearts. There are nights when I replay those memories and wish I had been more patient, more understanding, more mature. The “devil” that destroys me is the guilt that reminds me I once chose myself at the expense of others. But facing that truth is also what changed me. I had to accept that I was wrong. I had to swallow my pride and admit that love is not about winning arguments or proving a point. It’s about protecting the hearts of the people you care about. Growth came when I started listening instead of interrupting, apologizing without excuses, and changing without demanding recognition for it. I still carry the memory of that old version of me, but I don’t let it control me anymore. Instead, I let it remind me of who I never want to become again. The devil that once destroyed my relationships is now the reason I fight to be better—more patient, more selfless, more aware. I cannot erase my past, but I can choose every day to love people in a way that heals instead of harms.
